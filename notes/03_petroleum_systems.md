# Petroleum systems fundamentals

Short technical notes on petroleum systems analysis, written while
refreshing basin analysis and petroleum geology fundamentals.

## The five elements

- **Source rock** — organic-matter-rich rock (typically shale) with
  the potential to generate hydrocarbons once sufficiently matured.
- **Reservoir rock** — porous and permeable rock capable of storing
  hydrocarbons (sandstone, carbonate).
- **Seal / cap rock** — impermeable rock that prevents hydrocarbons
  from escaping upward or laterally out of the reservoir.
- **Trap** — a geological configuration (structural, stratigraphic,
  or combination) capable of holding migrating hydrocarbons in place.
- **Migration path** — the route hydrocarbons travel from the source
  rock to the trap.

## The two critical processes

- **Maturation** — organic matter in the source rock is converted to
  hydrocarbons through sufficient heat and burial time.
- **Timing** — the trap must form *before* hydrocarbon migration
  occurs. A structurally perfect trap that forms after expulsion is a
  failed petroleum system: the hydrocarbons had nowhere to go and
  simply escaped.

## Migration mechanism

Hydrocarbons migrate upward primarily due to buoyancy: they are less
dense than the surrounding formation water, so they rise until
stopped by a seal or trap geometry, or until they reach the surface
as a seep.

## Vitrinite reflectance as a maturity indicator

Vitrinite is a maceral derived from land-plant organic matter,
commonly disseminated within source rocks (not limited to coal seams).
Heat and burial progressively and irreversibly alter its molecular
structure, increasing the percentage of light it reflects (%Ro). This
makes %Ro a standard proxy for thermal maturity — roughly 0.6-1.3% Ro
corresponds to the classic "oil window."

## CRS (common risk segment) mapping

Rather than assessing petroleum system elements in isolation, CRS
mapping layers individual element maps (source presence/quality,
reservoir presence/quality, trap presence, etc.) to identify areas
where all elements coincide — the lowest-risk "sweet spots." This is
the core methodology behind regional risk products such as those sold
by Neftex.

## Relevance to the Thrace Basin project

TOC, pyrolysis, porosity, and vitrinite reflectance databases built
for Equinor and Valeura on Eocene-Oligocene deltaic/turbiditic flysch
systems map directly onto source rock quality and thermal maturity
assessment — two of the core inputs into petroleum system risk
evaluation.
